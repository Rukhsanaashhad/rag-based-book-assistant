Capstone Project

This module outlines the Capstone Project, which will bring together all the concepts and skills you have learned throughout this book.

Project Overview​

Goal and Objectives​

The goal of the Capstone Project is to design, build, and program an autonomous humanoid robot that can perform a complex task. The specific objectives of the project will be determined by you, in consultation with your instructor.

Scope and Deliverables​

The scope of the project will depend on the specific task you choose. However, all projects will be expected to include the following deliverables:

  • A project proposal
  • A final report
  • A live demonstration of your robot

System Architecture​

Hardware Components​

You will be responsible for selecting and integrating the hardware components for your robot. This may include a humanoid robot kit, sensors, actuators, and a computer for running your software.

Software Components​

You will be using ROS 2, Gazebo or Isaac Sim, and other software tools to develop the software for your robot. You will also be expected to write your own custom software for perception, planning, and control.

Development Roadmap​

Phase 1: Simulation and Prototyping​

In the first phase of the project, you will develop and test your robot in simulation. This will allow you to iterate quickly on your design and to test your software without the risk of damaging your hardware.

Phase 2: Hardware Integration and Testing​

In the second phase of the project, you will integrate your software with your physical robot. This will involve testing your software on the real robot and debugging any issues that arise.

Phase 3: Final Demonstration and Evaluation​

In the final phase of the project, you will demonstrate your robot's ability to perform the chosen task. Your project will be evaluated based on the performance of your robot, the quality of your final report, and your presentation.

Assessment and Grading​

Project Proposal​

Your project proposal will be graded based on the clarity of your project goals, the feasibility of your proposed solution, and the quality of your writing.

Final Report and Presentation​

Your final report and presentation will be graded based on the quality of your work, the clarity of your communication, and your ability to demonstrate your robot's capabilities.

Practice Exercises

  1. Capstone Project Proposal: Draft a detailed proposal for your Capstone Project. Clearly articulate the specific problem your autonomous humanoid will solve, outline the core functionalities, and define measurable success criteria.
  2. System Architecture Diagram: Create a high-level system architecture diagram for your Capstone Project, illustrating the flow of information between different components (e.g., perception, planning, control, human-robot interface) and identifying the key technologies used.
  3. Task Breakdown and Dependency Analysis: For your chosen Capstone task, perform a detailed task breakdown. Identify prerequisite sub-tasks, parallelizable operations, and critical decision points within the robot's execution flow.
  4. Risk Assessment and Mitigation: Identify at least three major technical risks for your Capstone Project (e.g., "sim-to-real" gap, integration challenges, performance bottlenecks). Propose concrete mitigation strategies for each.
  5. Test Plan Development: Develop a comprehensive test plan for your Capstone Project. Include unit tests for individual components, integration tests for system-level functionality, and end-to-end acceptance tests for the overall task.

Quiz Questions

  1. What is the main purpose of a Capstone Project in an educational robotics curriculum?

    Details

    Answer The main purpose is to provide students with an opportunity to integrate and apply knowledge and skills acquired throughout their studies to design, implement, and demonstrate a complex, autonomous robotics system.

  2. Name two specific challenges you might encounter when developing a Capstone Project involving an autonomous humanoid robot.

    Details

    Answer Challenges include complex kinematics and dynamics, maintaining balance, robust perception in dynamic environments, integrating multimodal AI models, human-robot interaction design, and bridging the sim-to-real gap.

  3. How can simulation tools like NVIDIA Isaac Sim or Gazebo be effectively leveraged during the Capstone Project?

    Details

    Answer Simulation tools allow for safe and rapid prototyping, testing of complex algorithms without physical hardware, synthetic data generation for AI training, debugging, and iterative development before deploying to a physical robot.

  4. What is the role of a "design document" in the Capstone Project deliverables?

    Details

    Answer A design document formally outlines the project's architecture, chosen algorithms, implementation details, and overall strategy. It serves as a blueprint for development, ensures clarity, and facilitates effective communication among team members.

  5. Why is the ability to understand and respond to natural language (conversational robotics) an important feature for an autonomous humanoid Capstone Project?

    Details

    Answer It enhances the human-robot interaction, allowing users to give high-level, intuitive commands without specialized programming knowledge, making the robot more accessible, user-friendly, and capable of operating in diverse human environments.
